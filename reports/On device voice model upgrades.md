# Swap the brain first, then the ears

The best A/B candidate in the 2026 crop is **Gemma 4 E2B-it (April 2026, Apache 2.0)** as the chat LLM. The LLM is where the heat comes from, and E2B is the only swap that plausibly cuts that heat while keeping Gemma's persona reputation and adding a native system role. Its vendor-measured CPU decode is **46.9 tok/s on an S26 Ultra under LiteRT-LM**, against **17.7 tok/s for E4B**. Test it against **Ministral 3 3B Instruct 2512**, which uses plain dense attention and has no context-shift problems. Two nearly free drop-ins should ship alongside that test. **Silero VAD v6** raises noise rejection on ESC-50 from 0.61 (v5) to 0.87 by Silero's own benchmark. **Pipecat Smart Turn v3.x** is an 8 MB, BSD-2 end-of-turn model and could cut the 1.2 s silence wait to roughly half a second. For TTS, the only new model worth phone time is **Kyutai Pocket TTS** (100M, streaming, voice cloning, already in sherpa-onnx), but its weights license is contradictory across sources and must be settled first. For ASR, **NVIDIA Nemotron Speech Streaming EN 0.6B** could replace both passes at 7.07% average WER. It is also a 600M encoder running continuously, the opposite of what a heat-limited phone wants, so the lighter **Moonshine v2 Small** (123M, 7.84%) should be benched first. For memory, **EmbeddingGemma-300M** remains the default and **Granite Embedding 97M multilingual R2** is the low-heat challenger. **Nemotron 3 Diarization** is feasible on Android but is the wrong tool for self-voice rejection. A speaker-embedding veto against the companion's own TTS voice is cheaper and more direct. Almost no phone-CPU numbers exist for any of these models on a Snapdragon 8 Gen 3. Every speed figure below comes from a different device or runtime, so the in-house bench decides.

## Gemma 4 E2B is the persona swap most likely to cool the phone

Five in-window model families compete at 1-4.5B. Only four of them plausibly match Gemma 3 4B on chat quality: Gemma 4 E2B/E4B, Qwen3.5 2B/4B, Ministral 3 3B and MiniCPM5-2B. Qwen3.6 and Qwen3.8 shipped no small models, so Qwen3.5 is still the latest small Qwen ([codersera, aggregator](https://codersera.com/blog/qwen-3-8-model-lineup-2026/)). Liquid's LFM2.5-1.2B is a speed and thermal option, not a quality upgrade.

| Candidate | Size / arch | Measured speed (not S24U, not llama.cpp unless noted) | Quality signal | License | llama.cpp caveat |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Gemma 4 E2B-it** | 2.3B effective / 5.1B total incl. per-layer embeddings; 512-token SWA + global; **native system role** ([HF](https://huggingface.co/google/gemma-4-E2B-it)) | S26U CPU LiteRT: **557 pp / 46.9 tg tok/s, 1.8 s TTFT, 1,733 MB** ([HF LiteRT](https://huggingface.co/litert-community/gemma-4-E2B-it-litert-lm)) | MMLU-Pro 60.0 | Apache 2.0 | Context shift broken ([#21379](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/issues/21379)) |
| **Ministral 3 3B Instruct 2512** | 3.4B dense + 0.4B vision; "strong adherence" to system prompts ([HF GGUF](https://huggingface.co/mistralai/Ministral-3-3B-Instruct-2512-GGUF)) | None published | No persona data | Apache 2.0 | Dense, so shift should work; official Q4_K_M |
| **Gemma 4 E4B-it** (quality ceiling) | 4.5B effective / 8B total ([HF](https://huggingface.co/google/gemma-4-E4B)) | S26U CPU LiteRT: **195 pp / 17.7 tg, 5.3 s TTFT, 3,283 MB** ([HF LiteRT](https://huggingface.co/litert-community/gemma-4-E4B-it-litert-lm)) | MMLU-Pro 69.4 | Apache 2.0 | Same SWA issue |
| Qwen3.5-4B (thinking off) | Gated DeltaNet hybrid, thinking **on by default** ([HF](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-4B)) | ~3 GB at Q4 ([AA](https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/qwen3-5-small-models)) | **IFEval 89.8**, but **80% hallucination rate** ([AA](https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/qwen3-5-small-models)) | Apache 2.0 | No KV shift ([#24944](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/discussions/24944)) |
| LFM2.5-1.2B-Instruct (S20+ fallback) | 1.17B conv/GQA hybrid, 32K ctx | S25U CPU: **335 pp / 70 tg, 719 MB** ([Liquid](https://www.liquid.ai/blog/introducing-lfm2-5-the-next-generation-of-on-device-ai)) | IFEval 86.2, MMLU-Pro 44.4 | LFM Open License 1.0, not Apache | Official GGUF |

The case for **Gemma 4 E2B** rests on three points. It inherits the family with the best community reputation for roleplay at small sizes, though that evidence is anecdotal and mostly about the 31B ([XDA](https://www.xda-developers.com/ran-gemma-4-and-qwen-35-for-same-local-tasks-one-pulled-ahead/)). It finally offers a real system role, which should help persona adherence and the "[happy]" tag prefill. And its speed is roughly 2.6x E4B's on the same hardware. Scaled down two chip generations and moved from LiteRT to llama.cpp, a plausible S24U decode is about 20-30 tok/s. That is an estimate, not a measurement. One community source claims 15-25 tok/s on phones in llama.cpp, which is unverified ([Lushbinary](https://lushbinary.com/blog/gemma-4-edge-deployment-mobile-iot-on-device-ai-guide/)).

The main risk is KV management. llama.cpp logs "forcing full prompt re-processing" for Gemma 4 because of SWA or hybrid memory, and the issue was still open at research time ([llama.cpp #21379](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/issues/21379)). `--cache-reuse` is also reported unsupported ([#21468](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/issues/21468)). This is the same class of problem the app already handles for Gemma 3, so the existing truncate-and-re-prefill path should carry over. Two things are **unverified**: the real Q4_K_M file size and RSS in llama.cpp, since the per-layer embedding tables inflate "5.1B total", and whether the embedded Jinja template exposes the system role correctly.

**Ministral 3 3B** is the clean-architecture control. It uses dense attention, has an official Mistral Q4_K_M, and should need no special cache handling. At 3.4B dense, though, it will likely run at about the speed of the Llama 3.2 3B the project already tried, so it wins only on persona, not on heat. **E4B** probably does not fix thermals, and 3.3 GB of working memory is risky on the 8 GB S20+. **Qwen3.5-4B** has the best instruction-following score, but it comes from the same lineage that spammed emoji in the project's Qwen3 4B 2507 test. It also defaults to thinking, which must be forced off, and its recommended presence_penalty is 1.5 ([HF](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-4B)). Treat it as a third-tier arm. **MiniCPM5-2B** is a dark horse to hold in reserve: plain `LlamaForCausalLM`, IFEval 86.7, Apache 2.0, official GGUF ([HF](https://huggingface.co/openbmb/MiniCPM5-2B)). Its release date is unconfirmed.

No public benchmark measures persona warmth, character drift or emotion-tag compliance for any sub-5B model in this window. EQ-Bench data did not render for scraping. IFEval scores in the high 80s predict that memory-note distillation will work, and say nothing about heat or charm. **First test:** run `persona_eval`, `drift_eval` and `recall_eval` on the Mac harness with identical replayed conversations for Gemma 3 4B (baseline), Gemma 4 E2B, Ministral 3 3B and Gemma 4 E4B, all at Q4_K_M, with the real system prompt and "[happy]" prefill and thinking forced off. Log emoji and markdown rate, mean reply length and tag compliance. On the S24U, run `llama-bench -p 512 -n 128 -t 4` plus a 10-minute sustained-generation loop per GGUF to capture thermal decay. Replay one long conversation to exercise Gemma 4's context-full path.

## Two cheap swaps shorten turns; a 600M streaming encoder is the heat gamble

### VAD and end-of-turn: the highest return per watt in the whole stack

**Silero VAD v6** is the cheapest fix for false triggers. Silero's own benchmark gives noise-only accuracy of **0.87 on ESC-50 (v5 0.61, TEN VAD 0.42)** and **0.71 on a private noise set (v5 0.44, TEN VAD 0.47)**, with multi-domain ROC-AUC 0.97 ([Silero wiki](https://github.com/snakers4/silero-vad/wiki/Quality-Metrics)). The benchmark is vendor-run, and TEN's promoters claim the opposite for noisy scenes ([Communeify](https://www.communeify.com/en/blog/ten-vad-webrtc-killer-opensource-ai-voice-detection/)). v6 should still be the default, with TEN VAD as the only alternative worth a replay. The v6 release date, and whether sherpa-onnx's bundled `silero_vad.onnx` is already v6, are unconfirmed.

**Pipecat Smart Turn v3.1/v3.2** is the end-of-turn pick. It is a Whisper-Tiny encoder plus a linear head, about 8M parameters, **8 MB int8 ONNX, BSD-2**, and is designed to run only when VAD reports silence ([GitHub](https://github.com/pipecat-ai/smart-turn)). v3.1 reaches **94.7% English accuracy at 37 ms on an AWS c7a.medium CPU** (8 MB model), up from 88.3% for v3.0 ([Daily](https://www.daily.co/blog/improved-accuracy-in-smart-turn-v3-1/)). One inference per pause costs almost nothing in heat. The alternative, **LiveKit Turn Detector v1** (June 2026), fuses audio and semantics. It reaches **5% false cutoffs at 543 ms mean latency and 10% at 295 ms** ([LiveKit](https://livekit.com/blog/solving-end-of-turn-detection)). Those numbers are for the cloud v1, not the local v1-mini, and the weights are under the restrictive LiveKit Model License, so it needs legal review before it can be anything but a reference. Smart Turn publishes only a single accuracy point and no cutoff-versus-latency curve, so the in-house test has to draw that curve. **First test:** run Smart Turn on every 250 ms pause in recorded conversations and plot false-cutoff rate against mean end-of-turn latency, compared with fixed 0.8 s and 1.2 s rules. Target at most 5% false cutoffs at no more than 500 ms mean. If it passes, fold it into the existing 250 ms speculative start.

### ASR: one streaming model could replace both passes, if the phone can afford it

| Candidate | Params | Avg Open-ASR WER | sherpa-onnx status | License |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Nemotron Speech Streaming EN 0.6B** (Jan 2026, updated Mar 2026) | 600M cache-aware FastConformer-RNNT | **7.07% @ 560 ms**, 7.67% @ 160 ms, 6.93% @ 1.12 s ([HF](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/nemotron-speech-streaming-en-0.6b)) | `...-560ms-int8-2026-04-25` with Android APK ([docs](https://k2-fsa.github.io/sherpa/onnx/nemo/index.html)); greedy only ([#3572](https://github.com/k2-fsa/sherpa-onnx/issues/3572)) | NVIDIA Open Model License |
| **Moonshine v2 Small / Medium** (Feb 2026) | 123M / 245M, sliding-window streaming encoder | **7.84% / 6.65%**; M3 CPU latency 148 ms / 258 ms ([arXiv 2602.12241](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.12241v1)) | Reported supported; **unverified** ([docs](https://k2-fsa.github.io/sherpa/onnx/moonshine/index.html)) | "Permissive"; exact license unconfirmed |
| **Parakeet Unified EN 0.6B** (Apr 2026), as pass 2 | 600M RNNT | **5.91% offline**, 6.52% @ 560 ms ([HF](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/parakeet-unified-en-0.6b)) | Non-streaming export only ([#3573](https://github.com/k2-fsa/sherpa-onnx/issues/3573)) | NVIDIA Open Model License |

Cache-aware streaming barely loses accuracy relative to offline decoding. Nemotron moves only 0.14 points between 560 ms and 1.12 s chunks, and int4 k-quant under ONNX Runtime keeps LibriSpeech-clean within about 0.05 points while shrinking the model 73% to 0.67 GB. Chunked offline models, by contrast, fall apart: Qwen3-ASR-1.7B goes from 5.90% to 10.45% ([arXiv 2604.14493](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.14493v1)). That paper reports only x86 speed. **Nemotron-only** would remove the 171-300 ms pass-2 step and give punctuated finals about one chunk after speech ends. The cost is running a 600M encoder continuously, against roughly 70M for the 2023 Zipformer, which is about 5-10x the compute per second of audio by parameter count. The sherpa export also ships only 560 ms chunks, coarser than today's partials. **Moonshine v2 Small** delivers accuracy close to Nemotron at 160 ms with a fifth of the parameters, which makes it the heat-sane first arm. **Parakeet Unified** is a same-size drop-in for pass 2 with a better offline score. Parakeet TDT v3 is multilingual and is not expected to beat v2 on English. The leaderboard leaders (Granite Speech 4.1 2B, Canary-Qwen 2.5B, Voxtral Realtime 4B, Kyutai STT) are GPU-class and out of scope ([MarkTechPost](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/07/23/best-open-speech-recognition-asr-models-in-2026-wer-languages-latency-and-license-compared/)). No Android RTF exists for any of these models. **First test:** on the desktop, run the Nemotron int8 export and Moonshine v2 Small/Medium on the phone-degraded utterance set, scoring WER against Parakeet v2 and counting insertions in silence (the "And" artifact). Then measure 10-minute sustained RTF on the S24U at 4 threads while logging big-core frequency. Treat sustained RTF above about 0.3-0.4 for Nemotron as disqualifying. The arms are: current setup, Nemotron-only, Moonshine Small plus Parakeet v2 pass 2, and Zipformer plus Parakeet Unified pass 2.

## Pocket TTS is the only new voice worth phone time, once its license clears

Supertonic 3 (released 29 April 2026) is still the newest Supertonic. It has about 99M parameters, 6 preset voices, and inline non-verbal tags such as `<laugh>`, `<breath>` and `<sigh>`. It does not stream, and its **Voice Builder for custom voices shuts down after 31 August 2026**, which leaves the app with the 6 presets ([README](https://github.com/supertone-inc/supertonic/blob/main/README.md)). Its model license is OpenRAIL-M, which carries use-based restrictions ([HF](https://huggingface.co/Supertone/supertonic-3)).

**Kyutai Pocket TTS** (13 January 2026) is the strongest complement. It has 100M parameters, streams output with **about 200 ms to first chunk and RTF about 0.17 on a MacBook Air M4 using 2 cores**, clones a voice from a WAV, and exports voice states for reuse ([HF](https://huggingface.co/kyutai/pocket-tts); [GitHub](https://github.com/kyutai-labs/pocket-tts)). sherpa-onnx ships `sherpa-onnx-pocket-tts-int8-2026-01-26`, a C API example and an Android Compose cloning demo ([HF](https://huggingface.co/csukuangfj2/sherpa-onnx-pocket-tts-int8-2026-01-26); [PR #3773](https://github.com/k2-fsa/sherpa-onnx/pull/3773)). A competitor's desktop benchmark puts Pocket's CPU cost at **0.37x core-hours and 610 MB peak, against Kokoro's 1.28x and 2.0 GB** ([Picovoice](https://picovoice.ai/blog/on-device-tts/)). Streaming matters more than raw RTF here: today's 2-word first chunk takes 1.0-1.3 s. Two flags stand out. First, the **license conflicts across sources**: MIT code with a prohibited-use list, CC-BY-4.0 weights per the HF card, "non-commercial" on the sherpa int8 card, and Apache-2.0 in a third-party app ([GitHub](https://github.com/kyutai-labs/pocket-tts); [sherpa card](https://huggingface.co/csukuangfj2/sherpa-onnx-pocket-tts-int8-2026-01-26)). Second, cloned voices sound different in sherpa-onnx than in the reference library ([#3180](https://github.com/k2-fsa/sherpa-onnx/issues/3180)).

No sub-150M CPU model offers true emotion-style tags. The practical route to the seven emotions is therefore reference-audio cloning: one cached Pocket voice state per character and emotion. Whether Pocket actually transfers emotion from the reference clip is unmeasured. **Kokoro 82M** stays the quality anchor, as the top sub-100M open model on the Artificial Analysis arena at Elo 1065 ([AA](https://artificialanalysis.ai/text-to-speech/leaderboard/provider-voice/open-weights)). **Kitten TTS 0.8** (14/40/80M, Mar 2026) is low priority. It has no emotion control, reported problems with numbers, and about 1.5x real-time on a desktop for the 80M ([Show HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47441546)). Soprano 80M has no ONNX or sherpa path. Chatterbox Turbo is the only small model whose emotion tags reportedly work, and only under ONNX ([#557](https://github.com/resemble-ai/chatterbox/issues/557)), but it needs 7.5 GB peak and 13.4x core-hours on a desktop. Along with VibeVoice, Qwen3-TTS and NeuTTS Air, treat it as a desktop reference only. **First test:** resolve the Pocket license by reading the HF repo's LICENSE file. Then bench the sherpa int8 build at 2 threads for RTF, streaming first-chunk time and Parakeet WER on the existing sentence set including numbers, against Supertonic 3 and Kokoro in the same session. Clone the three character voices from consented clips, and try 7 emotion clips for one character. In parallel, check that Supertonic 3's `<laugh>`/`<sigh>`/`<breath>` tags actually render through sherpa-onnx, which is the zero-cost emotion seasoning available today.

## Memory retrieval barely costs heat; self-voice rejection needs an embedding, not diarization

### Embeddings: pick on recall, because a few hundred notes are cheap to search

At under 1,000 notes, retrieval cost is noise next to the LLM. 500 notes at 256 dims is 512 KB, and brute-force cosine search needs no vector database, so recall should decide. **EmbeddingGemma-300M** is the default: 308M params, 768 dims with Matryoshka truncation to 128, under 200 MB RAM quantized, first-class llama.cpp and ONNX support, and MTEB 61.15 multilingual / 69.67 English ([HF blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/embeddinggemma); [BentoML](https://www.bentoml.com/blog/a-guide-to-open-source-embedding-models)). It needs the query and document prompt prefixes. Its license is described as "Apache 2.0 under Gemma terms", which conflicts with the usual Gemma Terms of Use and should be checked. **Granite Embedding Multilingual R2 97M** (May 2026) is the low-heat challenger. It scores **60.3 on MTEB multilingual retrieval, best open model under 100M**, ships ONNX, and is Apache 2.0 ([IBM](https://huggingface.co/blog/ibm-granite/granite-embedding-multilingual-r2)). **Bekko a25m** (Jul 2026) reaches 57.5 nDCG@10 with about 25M active params and is CC BY 4.0, but its int8 file is still 124 MiB for the 8M variant ([arXiv 2607.25180](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25180)). **Harrier-OSS-v1-270M** (MIT) posts a higher MTEB v2 mean of 66.5 but has no official GGUF or ONNX and no Matryoshka ([HF](https://huggingface.co/microsoft/harrier-oss-v1-270m)). Its score is an overall mean, not directly comparable to the retrieval subsets above, so it belongs in round two. Keep BM25 as a free boost for exact names; static potion-retrieval-32M reaches only about 82% of MiniLM's retrieval score ([model2vec](https://github.com/MinishLab/model2vec)). **First test:** hand-write about 50 turn-to-note pairs and 30 near-duplicate pairs over roughly 300 notes. Score BM25, potion, Granite 97M, Bekko a25m and EmbeddingGemma at 768 and 256 dims on recall@3/5 and duplicate precision at thresholds 0.85-0.95. Time each query on one big core while Gemma decodes, and pick the smallest model within 3 points of the best recall@3.

### Diarization and barge-in: Nemotron 3 Diarization answers a different question

**Nemotron 3 Diarization** (23 September 2026) is a 100M streaming Sortformer for up to 8 speakers. It cuts DER about **40% relative to Streaming Sortformer v2.1** at 1.04 s latency, for example DIHARD III offline 12.73 vs 19.09, and is OpenMDW-1.1 licensed ([NVIDIA blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/nvidia/nemotron-diarization); [HF](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/Nemotron-3-Diarization)). NVIDIA supports only Linux on its own GPUs. Low-latency modes cost about 35-100x more compute per second than offline mode. A third-party NeMo-Speech.cpp fork ran streaming ASR plus Nemotron-3 diarization on a Dimensity 1300 phone at **RTF 0.60 on 2 cores, 375 MB peak RSS, 107 MB q8_0 GGUF**, but confirmed speaker turns **about 5 s behind the audio** ([nemo-x-asr-diarizer.cpp](https://github.com/vieenrose/nemo-x-asr-diarizer.cpp)). That is feasible, but it is far too slow for a barge-in decision, which needs to happen within about 200-500 ms ([runedge.ai](https://www.runedge.ai/blog/barge-in-interruption-handling-on-device-voice)). It also adds a second ggml runtime competing with the LLM for big cores. Keep it as an optional multi-person feature, not a barge-in helper.

The better tool follows from the fact that the companion's voice is a fixed synthetic voice. Enroll it with a sherpa-onnx speaker-embedding model such as **titanet-small (192-dim, about 40 MB)** or **3D-Speaker CAM++**, both with Android APKs ([sherpa-onnx](https://k2-fsa.github.io/sherpa/onnx/speaker-identification/apk.html)). At each candidate barge-in, embed the last second of post-AEC mic audio and veto the barge-in if it matches. This runs only a few times a minute. The weakness is double-talk, where mixed audio makes embedding scores unreliable. Per-frame personal VAD and own-voice cancellation ("Don't Listen to Me", Interspeech 2026) address that, but no released phone-ready weights were confirmed ([arXiv 2606.23332](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.23332)). If the veto falls short, **DTLN-aec** (10.3M params, TFLite, 8 ms frames) can use the app's own TTS PCM as the far-end reference ([paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.14337)). It adds continuous CPU load, and chaining it after the platform AEC is untested. **First test:** record 20 minutes on the S24 with platform AEC on, covering companion-alone, user-interrupting and user-alone speech. Extract titanet-small and CAM++ scores at each VAD trigger, set T_self for 1% false vetoes of real barge-ins, and measure how many false barge-ins it removes combined with the existing text-overlap check.

## Prioritized test plan under a heat budget

The order follows one rule: test first what either removes heat or costs none, and let persona quality, not leaderboard deltas, break ties.

| Priority | Test | Why now | Heat effect | Gate to promote |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Chat LLM harness**: Gemma 3 4B vs **Gemma 4 E2B** vs **Ministral 3 3B** (E4B as ceiling) | LLM is the main heat source and persona is the top criterion | E2B plausibly cuts decode cost about 2x | Persona/drift/recall no worse than baseline; sustained S24U tok/s and 10-min thermal curve better; context-full path works |
| 2 | **Silero v6** file swap + noise replay | Minutes of work | None | Fewer VAD-open events and "And" insertions on fan/street/TV audio |
| 3 | **Smart Turn v3.x** on 250 ms pauses | Largest perceived-latency win for about 40 ms of compute | Negligible | ≤5% false cutoffs at ≤500 ms mean |
| 4 | **Self-voice embedding veto** (titanet-small / CAM++) | Fixes false barge-ins without a continuous model | Negligible (event-driven) | Removes most false barge-ins at 1% false vetoes |
| 5 | **Pocket TTS** vs Supertonic 3 vs Kokoro (after license check) | Streaming could cut first audio from about 1.2 s | Roughly neutral if phone RTF lands near 0.3-0.5 (speculative) | License clear; WER near Supertonic; blind preference; sustained RTF with LLM running |
| 6 | **Moonshine v2 Small** streaming, then **Nemotron EN 0.6B** | Better pass-1 accuracy; possibly drop pass 2 | Moonshine modest; Nemotron is a heat risk | Sustained RTF ≤0.3-0.4 and fewer silence insertions |
| 7 | **Parakeet Unified** as pass 2 | Same size as v2, better offline WER | Neutral | Lower WER at equal latency |
| 8 | **Embedding bake-off** (EmbeddingGemma vs Granite 97M vs Bekko) | Quality matters, cost does not | Negligible | Smallest model within 3 points of best recall@3 |
| 9 | **S20+ degradation**: LFM2.5-1.2B or Gemma 4 E2B, Moonshine Small, Supertonic | Confirms the 8 GB floor | Lowest-heat stack | Fits RAM with headroom; persona acceptable |
| 10 | Nemotron 3 Diarization, DTLN-aec | Optional, only if multi-person scenes or the veto fail | Adds continuous load | Diarization RTF <0.2; clear user benefit |

## Conclusion

The 2026 releases change the pipeline's economics more than its accuracy. Across ASR, TTS and turn-taking, the new models match what the app already has on paper, and they differ mainly in how much compute they spend per second of conversation. That favors event-driven helpers (Smart Turn, speaker-embedding veto) and streaming designs that cut latency without adding continuous load (Pocket TTS, Moonshine v2). It argues against always-on 600M encoders and 8-speaker diarizers. The LLM is the exception, where a smaller Gemma 4 could buy back heat directly. The deciding evidence there cannot come from any public benchmark, because nobody publishes persona-drift scores for 2B models.

The blind spot is the same in every section: no source measured any of these models on a Snapdragon 8 Gen 3 under sustained load next to a running LLM. Numbers from the S26 Ultra, M4, x86 and Dimensity phones set the order of tests, not the verdicts. Two items gate everything else: KV context handling for Gemma 4 in llama.cpp, and the Pocket TTS weights license. Check both before building on either model.
